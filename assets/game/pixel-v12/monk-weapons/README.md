# Monk weapons — pixel package v12

Weapon layers for the 110-key monk atlas (`pixel-v10/monk-side`): knuckles and staff (spec 3.8, question 5). A layer holds no skin, sash or hair pixels, so one layer serves all 18 appearances. Draw order: monk appearance atlas, then the weapon layer. Hands that grip the staff are already cut out of the staff layer, so the monk's own fists show over the shaft.

## Look

- Knuckles: a 2-row band across each visible hand, painted on skin pixels only (silhouette and hit mask unchanged): bright studs between bronze over a dark strap, at 62% of the hand's length along the forearm (front face of a fist, finger roots of an open hand). In a stance it is quiet, as gear board 4 expects; the hits carry the read: contact lights every stud and adds a 2×2 spark, the 4th strike a 5 px star with speed lines, then a bronze ring.
- Staff: 2u shaft, 3 px wood (light upper-left), 1 px ink rim, 3 px bronze caps, as on gear board 4. Held keys run it through both hands (rear hand = butt side), or along an authored angle when one hand shows. A staff behind the body (back views) is cut out of the body silhouette. On death it falls to the ground.
- Swings: the staff points along a ground azimuth and elevation from a grip on a reused monk body drawing (projection 32 px/u across, 16 px/u in depth, 32 px/u up), with a crescent smear along the tip's path from the previous key.

## Renderer integration

For a monk pose `{ frame, flip }` from `monkPose()`:

1. Knuckles, any clip: draw `knuckles.png` cell `frame` with the monk's anchor and flip. For strike `n` of the knuckle series use `knuckles.clips['knuckleStrike' + (n + 1)][front|back][key]`: draw monk key `frame`, then knuckles cell `overlay`.
2. Staff, non-attack clips and technique poses: draw `staff.png` cell `frame`, same anchor and flip. `keys[frame].staff` gives `grips` (hand centres, cell pixels), `butt`, `tip`, `angle` (degrees, screen space) and `depth` (+ in front of the body, − behind) for trails or hit sparks.
3. Staff strikes: `staffAttacks.clips['staffAttack' + (n + 1)][front|back|side][key]` gives `frame`, `mirrorBody` and `staff`. Draw monk key `frame` with flip `facing.flip !== mirrorBody`, then `staff-attacks.png` cell `staff` (160×144) with flip `facing.flip`. Both share the ground point: the swing anchor [80, 104] sits where the monk anchor [48, 84] sits (`monkCellOffset` [32, 20]). Flips mirror about the anchor x.
4. `keys[i].hands[]` (`centre`, `axis` wrist→knuckles, `pixels`, `knuckles`) serves effects; it is not needed to draw.

Filtering is nearest, no mipmaps, binary alpha, transparent RGB is zero. The layers are not part of the monk hit mask.

## How it was made

No image generation. `scripts/build-monk-weapons.py`:

1. Hand centres for keys 0–99 were authored from 4x/10x review sheets of the shipped atlas; the builder snaps each to its hand pixels (skin and its bronze/beads shading inside the ink, ≤4.5 px) and reads the forearm direction from the nearest wrap. The side keys reuse v2 upper bodies, so their hands are the source hands moved by `source/rig.json` `upperOffset`.
2. Bands, staff and swings are drawn from that data with the palette in `manifest.json` (ink, wood, metal, flare, smear).

## Commands

```sh
python3 scripts/build-monk-weapons.py                # rebuild the package
python3 scripts/build-monk-weapons.py --verify-only  # rebuild in memory, compare bytes and hashes, validate; no writes
node --test tests/monk-weapons-assets.test.js
```

Requirements: Python 3.10+, Pillow ≥10, <13. Preview: `artifacts/monk-weapons-preview.html` (served by the dev server).
